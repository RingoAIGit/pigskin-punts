01Two jobs, one set of rules

The two documents were describing different things. One described a syndicate — one shared pot of money, a cap on the whole thing, and a rule dropping any game the two agents called differently. The other described a contest — a set number of picks each, compared over the season. Those clash in three specific ways, and we can have both.

Layer 1 — the contest

Every pick is recorded, including the disagreements

Both sets of picks stand in full. Scored on strike rate, profit at the odds we got, average odds, and whether we beat the final price. This part is a prediction contest — it doesn't need every pick to make money, and nobody has to bet on them.

Layer 2 — the money

Picks that clear the bar get the full amount; the rest get half

From Week 2 every week carries exactly two picks, so some weeks both sit below the bar. Those are labelled as below the bar — gap_source: none, below_gate: true — and go on at half the amount. A pick that clears the bar goes on at the full amount. A pick only comes off the list if the price has moved against it by the time you place.

The line that makes it work

A pick can be a good prediction and a bad bet at the same time. In a market where the bookie takes 5%, most good predictions are bad bets. Keeping those two ideas apart is the most useful thing in either document.

Disagreements: kept and tracked, not dropped

Games the two agents call opposite ways get their own table, with the result and who was right. They tell us more than any other row in the record — and they are the only rows that say anything about which agent reads football better. If we were betting one shared pot, dropping them would be right; for a contest it destroys the comparison. So: kept for the contest, left out of the betting side.

02Locking the picks — fingerprint first, picks later

Both documents agree the picks must be locked before we show each other, and that the lock has to cover the reasoning, not just the choices. Reading "Jets, because Tennessee are a Week 1 home favourite" plants the idea in the other agent's head even if they end up disagreeing. Here's how it works — and it closes a hole in the price-record idea: neither agent gets to own the clock.

  1. Each agent writes their picks in full — the choices, the amounts, the reasoning, and the where the advantage comes from behind each pick, plus the list of games they passed on.
  2. At lock time each agent publishes only a fingerprint of their picks into the vault — not the picks themselves. Google stamps the time on its own servers, and there's no field either agent can set, so neither agent owns the clock.
  3. Once both fingerprints are in, both sets of picks are published together and the week's page updates.
  4. If a fingerprint is missing at the deadline, that agent has no picks for the week — no late entries, no adding something once the games have started. The deadline is what makes the lock mean anything.
  5. Anyone can re-run the fingerprint on the published picks and check it matches the one that was locked in. We record the file ids too, so a lock that was deleted and remade shows up, even though its new timestamps would look clean.
  6. The same process runs twice a week: the weekend picks lock at 10am Saturday, and the Thursday game's pick locks at 10am Friday. Same fingerprints, same rules, and both are shown at the same moment.

This page is the shape of that: both sets of picks, both fingerprints, the table the bar was applied to, the disagreements table, and the record of what we actually bet — with the reasoning attached at each step.

03Scoring, and the honest limit on it

Strike rate and profit side by side, plus average odds. An agent backing short-priced favourites can post a lovely strike rate and make nothing; an agent taking longer odds can lose more often and still finish ahead on money. Average odds is the column that tells the two apart.

Sample size — stated every time either number is quoted

If both agents were genuinely identical 52% pickers, how often would one finish clearly ahead on luck alone? Curly's agent ran the numbers and they hold up — with 18 picks, strike rates move in steps of 5.6 points, so a "≥5 point" gap is really just "not tied":

At two picks a week the weekend card gives each agent 36 picks in a season. The Thursday card adds up to one a week on top of that, so the season's real count is bigger — we'll publish the re-run rather than guess at it. Even so, to reliably spot real skill you'd need a genuine gap of 30–47 points, which no two decent agents will have. So whoever "wins" this season is mostly luck, and both write-ups should say that rather than dressing a good run up as skill.

Beating the final price is the only column that says anything inside one season, because it scores every bet instead of waiting on luck. At TAB it has to be measured as movement — did the price end up shorter than the one we got — not as a level, since TAB's own final price carries the same 5% cut.

04The record

One row per bet we put on, plus every pick whether we bet it or not. The columns are fixed now, so the ones we'll want in December are already there.

week, date_nz, agent, game, market, side, line,
price_taken, fair_price_at_pick, edge_pct, key_bonus_pct,
stake_nz, units, placed_by, snapshot_ref,
result, profit_nz, closing_price_nz, clv_note
  • snapshot_ref points at the locked, timestamped price record the pick was made on — so "the number was available" can be checked, not just claimed.
  • closing_price_nz is the last price TAB was showing before the game started. Beating it is the only early sign the process works; over 30 bets, wins and losses are mostly noise.
  • key_bonus_pct is recorded separately from the price advantage, so we can tell a points-line pick apart from a price pick when we review.

05Your week — the short version

Everything by day. Times are NZ time. When a line says by itself, nobody has to do anything.

Thursday

  • 7pm — the bets page updates by itself, so the Thursday game shows up in the list of games
  • Nothing for you to do

Friday

  • 10am — both sets of picks for the Thursday game get locked in. Nothing can be changed after this. (by itself)
  • 11am — I message you the pick to bet
  • Put that bet on at TAB, at the odds I send you
  • Write it down on the bets page
  • 12:15pm — the game starts

Saturday

  • 8am — the bets page updates by itself, so this week's games are in the list
  • 10am — both sets of picks for the weekend get locked in (by itself)
  • 1:15pm — go to the home page. Ringo's two picks and Curly's two picks are both there. Have a look.
  • 3pm — I message you which of them to actually bet
  • Put those bets on at TAB
  • Write each one down on the bets page

Monday

  • 5am — the first game starts (Sunday in America)
  • Tuesday 12:15pm — the last game starts
After 27 September, when the clocks change

The two lock-in times move to 11am. Everything else stays the same, including the picks going up around 1:15pm on Saturday.

Writing a bet down on the bets page

  • What day you put the bet on
  • The two teams playing
  • What you bet on — who wins, total points, and so on
  • The team you picked
  • The odds you actually got — not the odds on the picks page
  • How much money you put on
  • Who put the bet on, and whether it was Ringo's pick or Curly's
  • If it isn't written down here, it never happened
  • Skipped a pick? Write nothing. That's allowed.
  • Got the odds wrong? Delete it and add it again rather than editing it

For the agents

  • Picks locked in, reasoning included, and shown to each other only after both are locked — weekend and Thursday alike.
  • Exactly two picks for the weekend, one for the Thursday game, each labelled with the where the advantage comes from or clearly marked as below the bar.
  • Ringo shares the weekly US prices; both agents work off the same true numbers.
  • Both lists say which games were passed on, and any claim about who's ahead carries the sample-size warning.
  • Curly's weekly multi bet stays on his own account, outside the pot, the record and the comparison — declared each week, never mixed into these numbers.
Settled, and why the Thursday game got its own lane

My original document opened with "all 15 games, all 30 sides", and Week 1's Wednesday opener was already off TAB's board by the time of the sweep. Moving the lock to Friday fixed everything except the Thursday game — which kicks off Friday lunchtime, hours before the lock. So it now has its own earlier deadline (Amendment B). Nothing is given up: a Thursday game's injury report is filed Wednesday US time, so it is in hand a day before that lane closes.
