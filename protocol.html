The card Bets Results Method

01Two layers, one set of machinery

The documents were describing different objects. One described a syndicate — a shared bankroll with a combined cap and a rule dropping any game the two agents called differently. The other described a contest — an agreed number of picks each, compared across the season. Those conflict in three specific ways, and both can be had.

Layer 1 — the contest

Every pick is recorded, including the disagreements

Both cards stand in full. Graded on hit rate, money at the price taken, average price taken, and CLV. This layer is a forecasting competition and does not require positive expected value — nobody is obliged to stake it.

Layer 2 — the money

Gate-clearing picks staked full, forced picks half

From Week 2 every card carries exactly two picks, so some weeks both sit below the gate. Those are labelled as forced — gap_source: none, below_gate: true — and go on at half stake. A pick that clears the gate goes on at full stake. A pick only comes off the card if the live price has moved against it by the time you place.

The line that makes it work

A pick can be a good forecast and a bad bet at the same time. In a 5%-margin market most good forecasts are bad bets. Keeping those two ideas apart is the most useful thing either document says.

Conflicts: kept and tracked, not dropped

Games the two agents call opposite ways go in their own table with the result and who was right. They're the highest-information rows in the ledger — and they are the only rows that can say anything about which agent reads football better. For a shared bankroll, dropping them is right; for a contest it destroys the comparison. So: tracked for the contest, excluded from staking under Layer 2.

02The seal — hashes first, text second

Both documents agree the cards must be sealed before exchange, and that sealing has to cover the reasoning, not just the selections. Reading "Jets, because Tennessee are a Week 1 home favourite" anchors the other agent even if they end up disagreeing. Here's the mechanism, and it fixes a hole in the current snapshot proposal: the timestamps must not belong to either competitor.

  1. Each agent writes their card in full — selections, staking, rationale, the gap_source per pick, and the passes list.
  2. At lock time each agent publishes only the SHA-256 hash of their card into the vault. Google assigns the timestamp server-side and the API exposes no field to set it, so neither agent owns the clock.
  3. Once both hashes are present, both full texts are published together and the week's page renders.
  4. A hash missing at the deadline voids that agent's card for the week — no card, no picks, no retro-fitting after the games start. The deadline is what makes the seal mean anything.
  5. Anyone can re-hash the published card and check it matches the sealed hash. The file ids are recorded too, so a deleted-and-recreated seal is caught even though its timestamps would look clean.
  6. Two lanes run the same machinery: the weekend card locks Friday 22:00 UTC, the Thursday game 24 hours earlier. Same hashes, same rules, both revealed together.

The page you're reading is the shape of that: both cards, both hashes, the edge table the gate ran on, the conflicts table, and the running ledger — with the reasoning attached at every step.

03Scoring, and the honest limit on it

Hit rate and money side by side, plus average price taken. An agent buying short favourites can post a fine hit rate and make nothing; an agent taking longer prices can lose more often and finish ahead on money. Average price is the column that tells the two styles apart.

Sample size — stated every time either number is quoted

If both agents are genuinely identical 52% pickers, how often does one finish clearly ahead on luck alone? Curly's agent simulated it, and the numbers hold up — at 18 picks a week's worth of hit rates move in steps of 5.6 points, so a "≥5 point" gap is really just "not tied":

At 1–2 picks a week a season produces 18–36 picks each. To reliably detect real skill at that size you'd need a true gap of 30–47 percentage points, which no two competent agents will have. So whoever "wins" this season is mostly luck, and both write-ups should say so rather than narrating a good run as skill.

CLV is the only column that can say anything inside one season, because it measures every bet continuously instead of as a coin flip. At TAB it has to be measured as movement — did the price end up shorter than what we took — not as a level, since TAB's own closing price carries the same five-percent margin.

04The ledger

One row per placed bet, plus every contest pick whether staked or not. Columns are fixed now so the columns we'll want in December exist in September.

week, date_nz, agent, game, market, side, line,
price_taken, fair_price_at_pick, edge_pct, key_bonus_pct,
stake_nz, units, placed_by, snapshot_ref,
result, profit_nz, closing_price_nz, clv_note
  • snapshot_ref points at the timestamped, immutable line snapshot the pick was made on — so "the number was available" is checkable, not asserted.
  • closing_price_nz is what TAB was showing at kickoff. Beating the close is the only early evidence the process works; over 30 bets, win/loss is mostly noise.
  • key_bonus_pct is recorded separately from the price edge, so a number-based play can be told apart from a price-based one when we review.

  2. Wait for the check, then place (a minute). Before each window closes you get a short PLACE / VOID line for each pick. Place the PLACE ones, skip the VOID ones. The check exists so you never have to price anything yourself.

The one rule that matters

The form is the only place a bet exists. A bet placed at TAB but not logged is invisible to the ledger, and the ledger is the only thing either agent can be graded on. If you skip a pick, log nothing for it — the sealed card already records that it was offered.

For the agents

  • Sealed cards including reasoning, exchanged only after both are locked — weekend card and Thursday card alike.
  • Exactly two picks on the weekend card, one on the Thursday card, each labelled with its gap_source or explicitly marked as forced and below the gate.
  • Ringo shares the weekly US consensus output; both agents price off the same fair numbers.
  • Both cards carry an explicit passes list and a sample-size caveat on any claim about who is ahead.
  • Curly's weekly multi stays on his personal account, outside the contest bankroll, the ledger and the comparison — declared each week, never touching these numbers.
Settled, and why the Thursday game got its own lane

My original document opened with "all 15 games, all 30 sides", and Week 1's Wednesday opener was already off TAB's board by the time of the sweep. Moving the lock to Friday fixed everything except the Thursday game — which kicks off Friday lunchtime, hours before the lock. So it now has its own earlier deadline (Amendment B). Nothing is given up: a Thursday game's injury report is filed Wednesday US time, so it is in hand a day before that lane closes.
