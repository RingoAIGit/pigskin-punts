The method

One question, asked honestly every week: is TAB's price longer than the true fair price? Everything below is machinery for answering it.

The card Bets Results Method

01What we're actually trying to beat

TAB is the only legal sportsbook in New Zealand. No other operator may offer sports betting to people located here, which has one hard consequence: there is no line shopping.

We can't beat the bookmaker's margin by taking the best price across five books, because there is only one book. Its margin is the tax we pay. Measured across this week's board, TAB runs 4.74% to 5.51%; the US market runs 2–3%.

So a bet at TAB starts ~2.5 points behind the US market before we've thought about the game at all. The only durable edge available is a stale or lazily copied number — TAB priced off a line the US market has already moved past.

02The gate

A side qualifies at an edge of +4.0% or better against the US no-vig fair price. Below that, the margin eats it.

Step 1

Read TAB's own prices

TAB's site is a Vue app pulling odds from api.tab.co.nz as public persisted GraphQL queries — no login. We read the exact prices, never an aggregator's "best odds" page.

Step 2

Get the true price from the US

Median no-vig fair price across the major US books (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, BetRivers, Fanatics and others). Median rather than mean, so one outlier can't drag it.

Step 3

Compare, then decide

edge = TAB price ÷ US fair price − 1, plus the key-number adjustment on lines and totals. Under +4%, no bet — and saying so is a result.

03Where the edge lives: the number, not the price

Because TAB prices ~5% worse than fair on Head To Head, those cards will almost never clear the gate. The edge has to come from the number — TAB posting +4 when the market is +3.5 is a real advantage even at identical odds.

Half-point values, one-sided

These are empirical: regular-season games 2011–2025 conditioned on the line sitting within a point of the key. The value of a half point is P(the favourite wins by exactly k) — the one case where the same game pushes at one number and wins or loses at the other. Counting games where either team wins by k roughly doubles every figure and is wrong.

The rule that matters more than the table

The bonus applies only when the half point straddles the key — the two numbers are k−0.5 and k, or k and k+0.5. A move from +3.5 to +4 does not cross 3; it saves a four-point loss and is worth the 4-value, ~2.6%. Half points at 5, 8, 9 and 11–13 are worth little.

04Bankroll and staking

Flagged, not hidden

NZ$5 on NZ$62 is 8% per unit

Conventional practice is 1–3%. An ordinary losing run of 6–8 units — which happens to good processes — takes the kitty to roughly NZ$25–35.

Two fixes, either is fine: top up to NZ$150–200 so NZ$5 is ~3%, or drop the unit to NZ$2 (~3.2%) with no new money. Both are pending — it's Neil's call, not the agents'.

05What this method can't do

Stated up front, so it doesn't get argued about later.

  • It cannot make a bad book good. A 5% margin is a 5% margin. Most weeks the correct card is small or empty.
  • It cannot out-model the market. We are not building a better rating system than a dozen US books with full-time staff. We're spotting where TAB's price is stale. Smaller edge, actually achievable.
  • The US median isn't gospel. It's the best free reference available. Where the US books disagree with each other by more than a point, confidence drops and a pass is better than a guess.
  • The de-vig method is still unsettled. Proportional or power changes every edge by up to 2.4 points. Until it's agreed and validated, treat the third decimal place as decoration.
  • In Week 1 there is no current-season form at all, and most teams haven't filed an injury report when lines post. Week 1 is a numbers exercise, not a football read.
