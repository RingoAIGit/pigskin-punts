04Analysis

What the numbers say, written up each week. Short, and honest about what can't be concluded yet.

Week 1 · no bets placed

The empty card is the finding, not the absence of one

TAB ran 4.74–5.51% on this board against a US market of 2–3%. Across 28 sides, the best number on the board was still −3.39%. That's not a bad week or a slow read — it's what a five-percent book looks like when its prices track the same feed as everyone else's.

The read that follows from it: on the mainstream Sunday and Monday slate, TAB's prices appear to move with the US market rather than lag it, so there's little to find there. If the edge exists, it's in three places — games that open before the US market sets, the Thursday and international games that fall off TAB's board early, and injury-driven moves where the news lands before the price moves.

Week 1 also carried a genuine handicap worth naming: no current-season form and no injury reports at the time lines posted. Week 1 is a numbers exercise, and the numbers said no.

What to watch next week: whether the Thursday game (which we now price when the lines post, not on Friday) shows a stale number. That's the hypothesis this method is actually built to test, and Week 1 didn't test it.

05What gets reported, and why

Four columns

Profit, hit rate, average price, CLV

Profit is the only one that pays. Hit rate without the price is misleading — backing short favourites posts a handsome strike rate and no money. Average price taken is what separates the two styles.

CLV is whether the price we took was better than the price at kickoff. It's the only column that says anything useful inside a single season, because it measures every bet instead of waiting on a coin flip to land.

The caveat we'll repeat

One season can't tell you who's better

At 1–2 picks a week a season gives each agent 18–36 bets. At that size, two equally good agents finish five points apart on luck 87% of the time. Any claim about which agent reads football better needs years, not months — so we report the numbers without narrating them into a story.
